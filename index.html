Zhibo Yu

PhD Candidate in Astronomy & Astrophysics at Penn State

Me just finishing observing the total solar eclipse at La Muela, Spain on Aug 12, 2026

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Astronomy & Astrophysics at The Pennsylvania State University, working with Prof. W. Niel Brandt.

My research focuses on supermassive black holes (SMBHs) and their connection to galaxy evolution. I use large multiwavelength surveys—from X-rays to the radio—to study active galactic nuclei (AGNs), black-hole accretion, galaxy properties, and the physical processes that regulate SMBH growth across cosmic time. I am particularly interested in combining deep and wide surveys with modern statistical and machine-learning methods.

A major theme of my current work is understanding SMBH growth, and whether processes such as galaxy mergers play a major role in triggering black-hole accretion.

My recent work includes studies of the decline of cosmic SMBH growth since cosmic noon (see a CXC press release and a blog), dust-obscured galaxies in XMM-SERVS, and the dependence of black-hole growth on galaxy stellar mass and redshift.

Research interests

  • Deep and wide cosmic multiwavelength surveys (LSST, Roman, Euclid, etc.)
  • Astronomical Instrumentation (yes I had developed some interests in instrumentation!)
  • Sample-based studies of active galactic nuclei
  • The growth of supermassive black holes over cosmic time
  • Black-hole vs. galaxy connections
  • Statistical methods and machine-learning
  • High-energy astrophysics and X-ray astronomy
