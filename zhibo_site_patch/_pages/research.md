## Supermassive black-hole growth across cosmic time

Supermassive black holes grow primarily through accretion, observed as active galactic nuclei (AGNs). My research aims to understand how the distribution of black-hole accretion changes with redshift and galaxy properties, and which physical mechanisms are responsible for the strong evolution in cosmic SMBH growth.

In recent work, I investigated the decline in SMBH growth from cosmic noon to the present day and found that the evolution of typical accretion rates is a key driver of this decline.

## Black-hole–galaxy coevolution

I study how SMBH accretion depends on host-galaxy properties such as stellar mass and star-formation activity. This work combines large galaxy samples with deep X-ray observations and probabilistic models of black-hole accretion.

A central goal is to connect measurements of AGN populations to the long-term coevolution of SMBHs and galaxies.

## Galaxy mergers and AGN triggering

One of my current projects asks a long-standing question: **How important are galaxy mergers for SMBH growth?**

I am combining realistic simulated galaxy images with deep and wide imaging from facilities including JWST and Euclid, using convolutional neural networks to identify merger signatures. The ultimate goal is not only to measure merger fractions, but to quantify the fraction of cosmic SMBH growth that occurs in merging galaxies.

## Dust-obscured galaxies

Dust-obscured galaxies (DOGs) are massive, dusty systems commonly found near cosmic noon. I have studied a large sample of DOGs in the XMM-SERVS fields using multiwavelength data from X-rays to radio wavelengths, with particular attention to their AGN content, star formation, and X-ray obscuration.

## Surveys and methods

My work makes use of wide and deep extragalactic surveys, including data from **Chandra, XMM-Newton, JWST, Euclid, Spitzer, Herschel, VLA**, and major optical/near-infrared surveys.

Methodologically, I work with:
- multiwavelength catalog construction and cross-matching;
- X-ray source populations and stacking;
- spectral-energy-distribution modeling;
- statistical models of black-hole accretion distributions;
- image simulations;
- convolutional neural networks for galaxy morphology.
